PRAVI practical guide · General information

GST reconciliation starts with reliable source data.

A reconciliation is a structured comparison—not just a matching exercise. It helps locate differences for investigation and gives the preparer a record of what was checked, what remains open and what needs a decision. This guide is general information, not a tax opinion or assurance conclusion.

1. Confirm the period and source

Fix the financial/tax period, registration or business unit in scope, accounting cut-off and the source files being compared. Keep an unchanged copy of the files used so the work can be reproduced.

2. Align the data before comparing

Use consistent invoice number, date, supplier/customer identifiers, taxable value, tax amounts, credit notes and amendments. Understand the fields and limitations of each source rather than assuming every file has identical coverage.

3. Prepare exception categories

Separate timing differences, missing invoices, duplicate entries, credit notes, amendments, classification issues and other mismatches. Avoid treating every mismatch as the same issue.

4. Assign follow-up and evidence

Record the difference, amount/period, reason if known, next action, owner, due date and supporting evidence. Where a difference depends on another party or interpretation, mark it for review.

5. Reconcile to the return preparation

Tie the final agreed schedules to the figures proposed for the relevant return, document changes after review and retain a record of open issues. Eligibility and treatment must be assessed against the facts and applicable law.

6. Close the loop

Set a recurring cut-off, hand-off date, review step and unresolved-item follow-up. Repeated exceptions may indicate a process problem that should be addressed upstream.

Important: This guide is general preparation material, not a legal opinion, tax opinion, audit, assurance, certification or confirmation that a specific statutory requirement applies. Verify the current requirements for the facts and period in question.

At a glance

Practical takeaways

  • Do not overwrite raw source exports without keeping a reproducible copy.
  • Document why an exception remains open; do not silently plug it.
  • Agree who prepares, reviews, approves and submits each item.
  • Treat this guide as preparation support, not confirmation of GST eligibility or legal compliance.
