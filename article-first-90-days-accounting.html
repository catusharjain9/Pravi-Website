Starting a business: the first 90 days of accounting

A practical foundation for setting up business records, responsibilities and a reliable first reporting cycle.

PRAVI BUSINESS ADVISORS LLPPractical guide

The first few months of a business create habits that can either make later reporting easier or leave a backlog of unexplained transactions. A simple, consistent process is usually more useful than a complicated system that nobody maintains.

1. Establish the record-keeping foundation

  • Confirm the legal entity, ownership details and bank accounts being used for business transactions.
  • Choose accounting software and define who can create, review and approve entries.
  • Set a consistent method for storing invoices, contracts, bank records and expense evidence.
  • Keep personal and business transactions distinguishable; document any founder-funded expenses or funds introduced.

2. Define a monthly close routine

Set a recurring date to collect records, post transactions, reconcile bank accounts and review major balances. Assign an owner to each step and track unresolved items instead of allowing them to disappear into email threads.

3. Track compliance obligations separately

Build an entity-specific calendar only after checking the registrations, activities, turnover, transaction types and other facts that determine which obligations apply. Record the due date, responsible person, evidence of completion and any open question. Avoid treating a generic checklist as confirmation that every listed filing applies.

4. Start reporting with a few useful numbers

At minimum, review available cash, overdue customer balances, supplier commitments, revenue and major expenses. Explain unusual movements and distinguish actual results from forecasts. As the business grows, expand the reporting pack based on decisions management needs to make.

5. Review the process after the first quarter

Identify repeated delays, recurring reconciliation differences and documents that are often missing. Improve the process at the source—such as invoice capture or approval ownership—rather than relying only on month-end corrections.

Practical takeaway: establish ownership, keep supporting documents organised, reconcile regularly and maintain a visible list of open items.

Questions to discuss with an adviser

  • Which registrations and recurring obligations apply to this entity and its activities?
  • Are accounting policies and expense classifications being applied consistently?
  • What management information is needed each month?
  • Which controls are proportionate to the current size of the business?
