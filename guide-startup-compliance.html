PRAVI practical guide · General information

Startup compliance begins by mapping the entity, activity and responsibility.

The requirements for a new business depend on its structure, activity, location, employees, transactions and regulatory environment. This guide helps organise an initial discussion; it is not a complete checklist for every entity and should not substitute for professional legal or tax advice.

1. Describe the business activity

Write down the products or services, where the business operates, who owns it, whether it employs staff, and any regulated activity. These facts influence the entity and registration questions to verify.

2. Confirm the entity route and ownership records

Document the proposed legal structure, founder/partner roles, decision rights and capital arrangements. Formal structuring and agreements should receive the appropriate legal and tax review.

3. Map registrations and licences

Identify candidate tax, entity and activity-based registrations. Verify which apply, who can submit the application and what evidence the relevant authority requires.

4. Establish books and supporting documents

Set up a reliable way to record transactions, store invoices, reconcile bank accounts, retain approvals and track money advanced by or owed to owners.

5. Create a calendar and assign owners

List recurring filings, event-based obligations and renewal dates. For each item, define the preparer, reviewer, approver, required inputs, submission responsibility and evidence to retain.

6. Build a basic reporting routine

Agree a monthly close date and a first reporting pack appropriate for the business. Early visibility into receivables, cash commitments, spending and compliance items can help owners ask better questions.

Important: This guide is general preparation material, not a legal opinion, tax opinion, audit, assurance, certification or confirmation that a specific statutory requirement applies. Verify the current requirements for the facts and period in question.

At a glance

Practical takeaways

  • Do not assume the same compliance list applies to every entity or business activity.
  • Keep a clear trail from transactions and approvals to the accounting records.
  • Use an agreed source of truth for due dates, notices and filing acknowledgements.
  • Obtain specialist advice where the activity or transaction requires it.
