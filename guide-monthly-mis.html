PRAVI practical guide · General information

A monthly MIS should answer the questions management actually has.

Management Information Systems reporting is more useful when the structure is stable from month to month and each number has a clear definition, source, owner and review date. A beautiful dashboard cannot compensate for incomplete or inconsistent accounting records.

1. Set the questions first

Choose the decisions or operating questions the report should support: performance, liquidity, collection cycles, cost movement, capacity or planned investment. The reporting pack should be shaped around the business rather than copying a template blindly.

2. Establish the monthly close

Define the transaction cut-off, bank and ledger reconciliations, key schedules, review responsibilities and the date by which reports can reasonably be produced.

3. Present the core financial picture

A pack may include income statement, balance sheet, cash-flow view, selected working-capital balances and notes on material movements. The scope depends on the available records and required accounting basis.

4. Explain variance, not only totals

Compare actuals with a relevant prior period or approved budget where those exist. Note the movement, the likely driver, the evidence available, the level of uncertainty and the follow-up required.

5. Track actions and ownership

Attach an open-action register for missing records, overdue reconciliations, collection items, approvals or management decisions. Assign an owner and target date instead of leaving the next step implicit.

6. Maintain consistent definitions

Define revenue, contribution, operating profit, working capital, overdue receivables and other selected measures. Changes in definition should be explained so comparisons are not misleading.

Important: This guide is general preparation material, not a legal opinion, tax opinion, audit, assurance, certification or confirmation that a specific statutory requirement applies. Verify the current requirements for the facts and period in question.

At a glance

Practical takeaways

  • Use only ratios and KPIs that have a clear definition and reliable input data.
  • Label estimates and assumptions; distinguish actuals from forecasts.
  • Keep reporting cadence and responsibilities realistic.
  • A reporting pack is a management aid, not an independent audit or assurance opinion.
