GST purchase reconciliation: how to organise mismatches

A structured workflow for comparing purchase records with available GSTR-2B data and documenting differences.

PRAVI BUSINESS ADVISORS LLPPractical guide

Purchase reconciliation is a review process, not simply a search for matching invoice numbers. Differences may arise from timing, data-entry issues, supplier reporting, credit notes, duplicate records or other transaction-specific circumstances. Each difference should be assessed using the available records and applicable GST rules.

1. Prepare clean source data

Collect the purchase register for the relevant period and the corresponding GSTR-2B data. Preserve source files and note their reporting periods. Standardise supplier GSTIN, document number, date, taxable value and tax amounts for comparison, without overwriting the original data.

2. Match using more than one field

Start with supplier GSTIN and document number, then compare document date, taxable value and tax amounts. Normalise obvious formatting differences in a separate comparison field, while retaining the original invoice reference for auditability. Avoid treating a fuzzy match as confirmed without review.

3. Classify the differences

  • Purchase register entry not located in the downloaded GSTR-2B data.
  • GSTR-2B entry not located in the purchase register.
  • Potential duplicate or repeated document.
  • Differences in invoice reference, date, taxable value or tax amount.
  • Credit note, amendment, timing or classification issue requiring investigation.

4. Assign evidence and ownership

For each material difference, record the source reference, reason if known, evidence required, person responsible and resolution status. Contact suppliers where appropriate and retain the response. Do not mark an item resolved merely because a note has been added.

5. Assess tax treatment separately

A data match alone does not establish eligibility for input tax credit. The applicable statutory conditions, restrictions, time limits and facts of the transaction must be reviewed for the relevant period. Escalate uncertain or material items for professional review before taking a filing position.

Practical takeaway: keep the original data, use repeatable matching rules, document exceptions and separate reconciliation status from the final tax conclusion.

What a useful reconciliation output contains

  • Summary of source records and period covered.
  • Matched records and unmatched items on each side.
  • Value and tax differences with clear categories.
  • Evidence, owner, status and review notes.
  • A final review trail before the relevant return or credit decision.
